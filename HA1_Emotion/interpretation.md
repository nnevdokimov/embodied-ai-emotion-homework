This analysis provides rung 2 evidence for a narrowly defined prediction task. We predicted published socialness ratings for sixteen words, leaving one word out at a time. The training mean achieved a mean absolute error of 0.659 rating points. A ridge model using vision, hearing, touch and action reached 0.725. Adding interoception increased the error to 0.771, a deterioration of 6.3 percent. This sample therefore failed the ten percent improvement criterion specified in HA0. Standardization used only the training words in each fold.

The simulator describes twenty nouns. Grounding averages seven feature bins, and specificity counts the shortest WordNet path to the root. We scale both axes across the inventory using min–max normalization. Seven words lie on the grounding midpoint. Three occupy the high–high quadrant and only one occupies the low–low quadrant. Ranking the same grounding means puts five words in the low–low quadrant. The midpoint cases show how strongly the apparent coverage depends on the scale.

Warmth denotes a thermal sensation. Averaging seven channels dilutes its strong touch rating. Weight changes can reverse its abstractness rank relative to emotion. We varied the tactile rating for ambiguous senses and compared rankings after standardization. The standardized axis is unbounded, and the rank correlation with baseline abstractness is 0.745.

The Spearman correlation between grounding and word frequency was 0.374, below the lecture threshold of 0.4. Frequency adjustment changed abstractness by at most 0.087 under min–max scaling.
