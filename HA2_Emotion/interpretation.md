This analysis provides rung 1 descriptive evidence about lexical category structure. We represent twenty noun senses and five connecting WordNet categories. Only category links enter the specificity calculations. Associations and metaphorical examples have separate labels and record their generated origin. The baseline withholds a known link to scar so that the effect of a missing annotation can be measured directly.

Depth changes substantially when intermediate WordNet categories are collapsed. Among nineteen reachable inventory nouns, compact depth and full WordNet depth have a Spearman correlation of 0.148. Warmth has nine original steps but only three displayed edges. Resentment is deeper than panic in WordNet, and their order reverses in the compact graph. Weighting every edge by the verified number of underlying hypernym steps recovers the HA1 depths for all nineteen nouns.

Restoring the parent of scar makes it reachable. The additional edge from emotion to panic creates paths of five and six displayed edges, giving normalized depth endpoints of 0.833 and 1.000. Both routes traverse seven underlying WordNet steps. This example demonstrates sensitivity to encoding an ancestor shortcut. Adding the association between anxiety and freeze leaves both estimates unchanged.

Removing the link from feeling to emotion alone disconnects eight nodes, reproducing the connectivity loss of the three deletions. Mood keeps its path, yet its normalized depth increases by one third because the maximum reachable depth falls from six to four. The fixed-scale comparison isolates this denominator effect.

Fifteen of nineteen reachable inventory nouns have no represented descendants and share the maximum breadth score. Adding three direct WordNet hyponyms lowers flood’s breadth score from 1.000 to 0.579. Broader categories such as feeling have many represented descendants, while narrow terms such as panic receive high scores under both estimates. Interpretation depends on the coverage of each branch and the units used for depth.
